# data tasks

The queue of data-layer work, each task many turns and the next ones many sessions. The three at the top run in a fixed order, and the order comes from what each proves about the one before it: the smaller dog can only remove Datadog once every change in the system has its record in ledger_table, and we can only be sure those records are complete once the tables have stopped keeping history of their own, because a fact a mutating table loses is lost only to that table and never to the ledger. So the ledgerathon first, then hideless, then the smaller dog. A new session takes each one, and this document with ledgerathon.md, hideless.md, and smaller-dog.md is what it reads to start. Live-table changes ride the proven migration flow — migration.md holds the playbook, each migration file and its SQL() registry edit in the same commit, grid tests beside code changes.

## ledgerathon

Next, and the first of the three in order above, planned in ledgerathon.md. A ledgerAdd beside every create, update, and delete in the system: every credential flow, sign-up and close account, and beyond our own tables the dealings with third parties no record covers yet, the upload lambda's storage call and the chain provider. The turnstile failure stays out: a failed bot check is not a change to our data but a record of our experience with a third party, what logAudit was for, and the smaller dog picks its channel, console.error to the Cloudflare dashboard or the one dog that survives, to Datadog. The function that mutates is the function that records, so no caller can forget, and the rule it installs is permanent: from the ledgerathon on, a new mutation arrives with its ledger row, in the same turn that writes it. It soaks for a week of real use before hideless deletes anything.

## hideless

Second, planned September 7, 2026 in hideless.md. Every table converts to editing cells and deleting rows, and the code around it with them: the hide column leaves every table, credential_table becomes one row per credential held or in flight, and level2 gains the delete and update verbs it was built not to have, on migration.md's playbook with a history backfill and collapse ahead of the code switch. It installs the tidiness rule: outside ledger_table, a table is the shortest possible statement of the system as it exists right now, with no history, and a finished flow leaves its table the moment the code knows. It is also the ledgerathon's proof: once a table deletes what it no longer holds, a mutation that left no ledger row is a loss that shows, where a hidden row would have covered for it. The hosted survey behind it: credential_table holds 292 rows and 221 are hidden, and no other table hides a row.

## smaller dog

Third, after the ledgerathon and hideless, because Datadog can only leave once every change in the system has its record in ledger_table and hideless has proven those records complete. Shrink the Datadog and logging apparatus to almost nothing, planned in smaller-dog.md. The ledgerAdd conversions that were to lead it are the ledgerathon's now: two are in — the message task writes its Challenged. row, tagged with the address type and the provider, at the worker while the lambda still sends its own audit, and both oauth sites write ledger rows beside the old audit — and the turnstile failure is a special case decided September 7, 2026: not a change to our data, so no ledger row and no layering decision, and this sprint picks its channel between console.error to the Cloudflare dashboard and the one dog that survives, to Datadog. What remains here is the removal pass: the function suite, the double-hulled door catches, the keepPromise parking lot, and the pluggable log sinks leave, and one async dog stays. The document holds the inventory, where each of the old four purposes goes, and the decisions the sprint must make — dog's destination, the attention channel, audit latency.

## proven rename

## backup plan

Researched and tabled, September 4, 2026, until much later. This was a research task, and the research is done: database-stack.md's backup-plan section records three good approaches — the pg_dump schema-and-data pair the CLI already half runs, the CSV cold copy down the scripts path, and Supabase's plan-gated managed backups — and what they establish is the confidence the sprint was after. We hold a variety of ways to back up, none of them ties us to Supabase as a vendor, and none of them needs the platform's turnkey and expensive options. Nothing waits on the choice, so it stays out of the queue's ordering, and it should not come up again until we deliberately pick it up. What remains for that day is the choosing: which combination, on what cadence, and where the sensitive files sleep, since a held backup is exactly as sensitive as the database — a fourth place secrets live, beside the workstation, the bundles, and the providers' secret services, so whatever encrypts one wants a home in the key system and a tracer family of its own.

## address and service table cleanup

Done August 26, 2026: address_table and service_table left the code, the schema, and the planning documents that mentioned them. service_table was the only one in the cloud — zero rows, nothing referencing it — and went by a single DROP paired with its registry edit; address_table was registry-only, so deleting its `SQL()` block was the whole schema change.
